Letter to the Editor

Green hydrogen to address seasonal variability of
wind and solar energy production in Australia

Keywords:       abstract
Energy storage
Green hydrogen  Energy storage is fundamental to tackling irregular energy production by solar and wind.
Australia       Unfortunately, the need for storage over different time scales is not properly acknowledged
Wind energy     in many jurisdictions, with Australia being one of them. A key research question to address
Solar energy    is how is it possible for Australia to move toward net zero producing energy only by solar
                and wind, compensating for the large seasonal variability. We show as without having
                dispatchable energy production using carbon and hydrocarbon fuels, or green hydrogen
                energy storage, this is impossible. The limited battery energy storage or pumped hydro
                energy storage being developed does not permit the necessary long-term storage in the
                huge amounts needed. Every serious approach to progress toward net-zero should include
                hydrogen and nuclear energy production. This perspective emphasizes the need for a
                balanced and pragmatic approach that considers a variety of energy sources and storage
                technologies to achieve both environmental and energy security goals.

                                                                      A holistic approach is needed to progress toward net-zero
                                                                  while maintaining a reliable energy supply. This involves a
                                                                  mix of renewable sources like solar and wind, coupled with
                                                                  various forms of energy storage including batteries, pumped
                                                                  hydro, green hydrogen, and nuclear power. The exact mix
                                                                  would depend on factors like geography, technological ad-
                                                                  vancements, societal preferences, and the future share of
                                                                  total energy by electricity or hydrogen.

                                                                      The energy landscape is complex, and there is ongoing
                                                                  debate and research about the most effective strategies for
                                                                  transitioning to net-zero emissions. Different regions might
                                                                  have different resource availability and priorities, so the
                                                                  optimal solutions could vary. Public policy, economic factors,
                                                                  and technological advancements will all play roles in shaping
                                                                  the energy transition. However, the need for energy storage
                                                                  and hydrogen, in addition to building up the capacity of wind
                                                                  and solar, is clear. Similarly needed is nuclear power.
